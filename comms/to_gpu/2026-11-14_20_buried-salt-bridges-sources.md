# 20: buried salt bridges: sources (addition to reply 18 and task 15, step 3)

From: cloud session, 14 November 2026, about 22:20. Justin asked for a literature source for report 15's +1.1 kT for a
buried salt bridge. Found (abstracts read; summarised, not quoted at length):

- **Hendsch and Tidor 1994,** *Protein Science* 3: 211, PMC2142793. Continuum electrostatics on 21 salt bridges in 9
  structures. Most (17) are destabilising compared with hydrophobic replacements of the same shape, by about
  3.5 kcal/mol (about 6 kT) on average. Their reading: the desolvation cost is not repaid. They propose that salt
  bridges serve specificity, by limiting the low-energy conformations.
- **Waldburger, Schildbach and Sauer 1995,** *Nature Structural Biology* 2: 122.
  - Arc repressor's buried salt-bridge triad (Arg31–Glu36–Arg40) can be replaced by hydrophobic residues. The mutants
    are significantly more stable, with comparable conformational specificity.
  - In a follow-up (PNAS 1996, PMC39681), forming the buried salt bridges is a slow, rate-limiting step in folding.
- **Kumar and Nussinov 1999** ("Salt bridge stability in monomeric proteins") and their 2002 review. 222 salt bridges
  in 36 proteins: most are stabilising, buried or exposed. Burial costs a large desolvation penalty, but the
  attraction is also stronger inside. The net depends strongly on geometry, and the same pair can switch between
  stabilising and destabilising as the protein flexes.
- **Thermophiles:** proteins from heat-loving organisms carry more salt bridges, and in the cases computed (Kumar et al.
  2000, glutamate dehydrogenase) they are strongly stabilising. Elcock 1998 (on salt-bridge stability at high
  temperature) is cited in this literature; not read.

**What this means for the table** (Claude's reading; proposals):
- **The sign is defensible.** A partnered buried salt bridge has a small net cost against an oily contact of the same
  size. That is the majority result of Hendsch and Tidor and of the Arc experiments; Kumar and Nussinov dissent.
- **Two benchmarks to check in step 3:**
  - a buried *unpartnered* charge should be far worse than a partnered pair (the Lehninger point);
  - the table's partnered pair should be worse than an oily contact by roughly 0–6 kT, depending on geometry.
- **Specificity:** salt bridges should narrow the set of low-energy folds even where they cost stability. This fits
  Justin's decision that polar and charged contacts carry specificity. A measure for step 3: whether sequences with
  buried pairs have fewer tied lowest-energy folds.
- **Heat:**
  - If the model makes water's screening depend on temperature, salt bridges could become a route to heat tolerance
    that organisms evolve, as in thermophiles.
  - Proposal only. Check a source before adding a temperature dependence for water's dielectric constant.
