| Quantity | Value | In kT |
|---|---|---|

| A covalent O–H bond | about 470 kJ/mol | |

| A covalent C–H bond | about 410 kJ/mol | |
| A typical van der Waals contact | as little as 4 kJ/mol | about 1.6 kT |

The strength of an ionic interaction goes as Q₁Q₂ over (ε r²). Water's ε is about 78.5 and benzene's about 4.6, so
the same pair attracts about 17 times more strongly in an oily environment. The chapter also notes that the
strengths of ionic interactions and hydrogen bonds vary with the solvent.

**Against report 15, section 1.2.**
- **Net contacts of −1.3 to −1.9 kT (3–5 kJ/mol): consistent.**
  - The 23 kJ/mol is the energy to break a hydrogen bond, not its net gain on folding. Chapter 4 says each hydrogen
    bond formed in a protein replaces one to water, so the net is small. A few kJ/mol net is the expected order, and
    comparable to a van der Waals contact.
- **The ratio to covalent bonds: consistent.** The table's 1–2.7 kT against a C–C bond of about 140 kT is a ratio of
  50–140, in line with the chapter's 4 against 350 kJ/mol.
- **A buried salt bridge at +1.1 kT: plausible in sign, not checked in size.**
  - Two effects pull in opposite directions. Burial raises the attraction about 17-fold (lower ε). It also costs the
    desolvation of two charges. Chapter 4 says an unpartnered charge buried in a core is strongly destabilising.
  - Chapter 2 gives no number for the balance. A net small cost for a partnered buried pair is a known result in the
    literature, but treat it as a proposal unless you have a source; cite it if so.
- **Nothing in chapter 2 contradicts the screening choice** (Debye–Hückel, salt bridge halved near 0.17 M). The chapter
  does not treat ionic strength numerically.
