# Theory notes: the systems model, Deleuze and Guattari's strata, and Hjelmslev's net

Notes from the web session, 11 November 2026. Everything here is a proposal unless marked as the user's decision. The
sources read were the passages of *A Thousand Plateaus*, plateau 3, on double articulation, proteins, nucleic acids,
infolding, the margin of decoding and side-communication.

## Decisions (the user's)

- The clump's code is called its **nucleus**. (Under review since 12 November: see "Biological accuracy comes first".)
- The protein step is called **infolding**. "Fold" keeps two meanings:
  - the user's operation that collapses a level;
  - the page's "Fold quiet regions" speed-up, which fits that sense.
- **Speciation and rudimentary evolution come before protists.**
- **Systems need not reproduce** (11 November). The model is meant to include any system, not only those that reproduce in
  the strict sense. A more general category is proposed by the user: **causal systems**, closed to efficient causation but
  not showing reproduction in the strict sense. Reproducing systems would be a kind of causal system.
- **Floor and ceiling are computational, not real** (11 November). They are the extremes of the range of systems being
  modelled at a given time. Lift and fold move them, elaborating the systems indefinitely. This corrects the proposal below
  that likened the floor to Deleuze and Guattari's substratum: the substratum is real for them, and the floor is not.
- **The subject at the human level** (11 November, tentative: "perhaps"). The subject or individual is perhaps a
  self-reproducing system at the intersection of a diverse set of biological and sociological systems, and it also
  mediates between the two groups of systems.
- **Causal systems negotiate; they are not closed per se** (11 November). This revises the item above. A causal
  system that comes first in time at first dominates a second. In time the second may become autonomous and
  "negotiate" with the first. Which system dominates is unclear, and it fluctuates.
  - **The core example: the user's disagreement with genetic determinism in sociobiology.** Biology must be considered
    within sociology. Genes may produce a society's particular mores, and a population's social norms may select genes.
- **Contagion probably has a cutoff** (11 November, a hypothesis). Beyond some divergence, horizontal transfer between
  species fails.
- **Biological accuracy comes first** (12 November), even where it means revising what is built. The first
  consequence, proposed and not yet confirmed: the clump's separate code is retired. Clump traits move into the cells'
  own sequences, soma and germ become regulation of one shared sequence, and "nucleus" is freed for the real organelle
  later. The plan is in the genetics doc (Proposal 3). **Confirmed the same day:** the revised order (domains; clump
  code retired; niches; selfish elements and viruses; endosymbiosis; nucleus; amoebas), "nucleus" for the real
  organelle, and spores renamed dormant cells. True spores are to evolve later from dormancy, and keep the word
  "spore".
- **Clump behaviour comes from what cells make** (12 November), not from named clump domains. Cells stick where they
  make a sticky molecule, and share when what they release feeds their neighbours. **You link this to the cellular
  layer:** the cell layer reads the molecular layer.
- **The cell layer comes with a view** (12 November): a visual representation of the cell layer, eventually showing
  specific characteristics such as its membrane and organelles.
  - **Proposal:** clicking a cell opens it, drawn from its actual state:
    - the membrane;
    - its domains as folded shapes;
    - the molecules it holds and their reactions;
    - its bonds to neighbours;
    - its light, and whether it is dormant.

    Compartments, a guest cell and the nucleus get drawn as each one is built. Opening the view is your open: it
    shows the cell layer only when you need it.
- **Semantic closure, the route (proposal, 12 November; your question).**
  1. **Behaviour read through products:** the step above. The material mode now stands between sequence and behaviour.
  2. **Adaptor domains:** the rule that turns a domain's shape into its reaction gets an evolvable part. A cell's
     adaptor domains reassign what a shape means, as aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases assign amino acids to codons. The fixed
     rule stays as the primordial chemistry when no adaptor acts. The code is then read by products of the code:
     Monod's point, cited by Deleuze and Guattari.
  3. **Prediction to test, the frozen accident:** once many domains depend on the code, changing it breaks them, so it
     should freeze. Small genomes should still be able to change it. Biology fits this: deviant codes exist, many in
     mitochondria, whose small genomes tolerate changes a larger system can't.
  4. **Readouts:** the diversity of codes among lineages, the rate at which the code changes, and changes against
     genome size.
- **The coat can be made from sugar** (12 November, option 1): a domain whose fold makes it a sugar-to-coat reaction,
  within reactions from shape. To be built as part of step 2.
- **Motility, first by gliding on slime** (12 November, your choice of option 1): cells that push out their coat
  creep. The flagellum is filed for a more realistic model later.
  - **Your observation:** the flagellum is unexpectedly complex, and its parts are used in other functions of the cell
    (it shares its export machinery with secretion systems). This shows why open-ended evolution is hard to model: it
    needs enough intricacy that similar parts can be reused in entirely different contexts.
  - **Note (proposal):** that is exaptation, and it is close to Taylor's condition of bridges between domains. The
    page's plan already holds one such reuse: the export fold planned for selfish stretches could later serve
    motility.
- **Swarming patterns** (your interest, 12 November): the branching colony patterns of swarming bacteria, such as the
  dish photo on Wikipedia.
  - **Proposal:** a dish world (no light, a low even nutrient, no currents, one seed in the centre), varying nutrient
    level and how easily cells move. **Checked:** for *Bacillus subtilis* these two knobs give a morphology diagram of
    distinct patterns: diffusion-limited branching, dense branching, round compact colonies, rings and discs.
- **The Game of Life rule is to be removed, both birth and survival** (12 November, your option 2). Cells would divide
  when they have stored enough energy and there is room, and die of real causes (starvation, drying, heat, predation).
  The compound keeps its role as what a cell is made of. Its neighbour-count primes (2 to 19) become ordinary
  building blocks with no special meaning, free to take on roles later through reactions (13 November, your decision).
  - **Filed for later (your note, 13 November):** the rule is worth keeping as an extension of Conway's game, for a
    possible separate, more playful artificial-life model. It does not belong in this one.
  - **Real analogues of a neighbour rule (checked):** none at the level of bacteria. The closest are in animal cells:
    contact inhibition of proliferation (cells stop dividing when hemmed in) and anoikis (cells die when they lose
    contact with their matrix or neighbours). In microbes, density effects arise through chemistry instead: the
    Allee effect of public goods, which the page could produce on its own.
- **Reactions from shape are the default** (12 November). The eight named enzyme jobs are off unless "Domains run
  reactions" is unticked. Energy comes only from vents and the dead.
  - **For the theory** (proposal): "a new closure brings a new code" holds for egalitarian unions, such as an
    endosymbiont that keeps its genome. It fails for fraternal ones, cells of one lineage, where the new level is the
    old code regulated: a second articulation.
- **Open choice: how the two models relate** (11 November). Either follow one model strictly, or separate the two precisely
  and say where each applies and how they relate. Not yet decided.

## Hjelmslev's net, mapped onto the page (proposal)

| Cell of the net | In the page |
|---|---|
| Substance of content | the matters a cell takes in and forms: the 16 molecules, sugar, water, CO₂ |
| Form of content | the order in which they are chosen: the compound, a product of primes (the user's handoff already places closure compositions here) |
| Form of expression | the ordered sequence (the handoff already places descriptions here) |
| Substance of expression | the monomers as letters of the sequence: the same 16 molecules, formed as expression |

- **Concern: the threshold.** Before the line of expression, the compound was both the code and the rule, so content and
  expression were not *really* distinct. Deleuze and Guattari make that real distinction, with expression detached as an
  independent linear sequence, the mark of the organic stratum. The page crossed it with the line of expression.
- **Double articulation inside content.** Deleuze and Guattari's example is protein: the infolded fibre plays the role of
  functional expression relative to the plain chain. The page has the same split:
  - the exposed monomers make the rule;
  - the buried pairs make the enzymes.
- **Concern: a term.** Deleuze and Guattari themselves put "overcoding", in scare quotes, on protein folding: the compact
  structure governing the aggregate. The user reserves "overcoding" for the human stratum. "Infolding" keeps the two
  apart, but the overlap should be kept in mind.

## Strata and the systems model (proposal)

- **Floor and ceiling.**
  - The floor is close to the *substratum*, which supplies materials and serves as milieu.
  - The ceiling is the largest closure that a stratum's distribution of content and expression supports.
- **Epistrata and parastrata.** These would be the user's two minimal supersystems (a proposal, not yet confirmed):
  - **Epistrata** are the milieus: interior, exterior, intermediate, annexed. This is the ecology, the closure of
    production.
  - **Parastrata** are forms, codes and populations. This is the lineage, the closure of reproduction.
- **Open.** The interior milieu takes over what was exterior. In the page, oxygen and sugar, made only by life, are open
  from the start; CO₂ and water open as life comes to supply most of them.
- **Lift.** A new closure brings a new code. The clump's nucleus is a code one level up: a parastratum of the clump.
- **Fold.** This would be molar aggregation: a statistical aggregate summarised as one process.
  - **Caution.** Deleuze and Guattari note that the line between the articulations sometimes coincides with the
    molecular/molar line and sometimes does not, so molar should not be assumed to be the fold.
- **The physico-chemical stratum** (closures without codes) is already in the page: the overturning water, iron rusting, the
  carbon cycle.

## Where the meshing breaks (concerns)

1. **No destratification.** Strata are captures, set against deterritorialisation, the body without organs and the plane
   of consistency. The systems model has nothing that leaves the strata. Its moving floor and ceiling are indefinite, not
   absolute.
2. **Lines of flight, only partly.** The model has them only as the margin of decoding and side-communication:
   - in the page now: duplications and contagion;
   - next: viruses.
3. **Reproduction-centred.** A system is defined by reproducing its subsets, but strata also hold orders that do not
   reproduce, such as the crystal. The physico-chemical stratum fits only if "reproduces its subsets" can include
   structures that merely maintain themselves.
4. **Readouts are not causes.** The open, lift and fold readouts measure crossings. They decide nothing. The page should
   stay that way.

## Findings so far that bear on the theory

- **Obligation without need.** Soma (cells that do not breed and feed the clump) is selected *against* unless the
  clump's closure needs it.
  - **Test to come:** soma's own enzymes shared with the clump, and stalk cells raising spores for dispersal in a social
    amoeba.
- **Protection without evolution.** Grazers shift cells into clumps by eating the free ones, but adhesion, clump size
  and soma do not evolve in response. This is unlike Herron and colleagues' 2019 experiment (checked).
- **Neutrality.** About 20% of copying errors leave the compound unchanged.
- **The error threshold does not apply.** Eigen's condition, `L < ln σ / p` (checked), assumes one best sequence. The
  page has many viable rules: many peaks, not one.
- **Passive lengthening.** Sequences lengthen even without a copying advantage, perhaps a passive trend from the floor at
  minimum length (Gould, McShea). The source is not yet checked, and a control is owed.
- **Enzymes follow the world's limits.** Uptake is selected strongly except where vents supply molecules, and light
  harvest is higher where light is scarce.
- **The nucleus's regulation of its soma** shows no signal yet.

## Overnight results, 11–12 November (measured; the readings are proposals)

Runs are on the "even" world unless noted, 3,000 ticks, with 2 to 4 trials each, so the counts are rough.

**1. Contagion that needs a matching stretch (your "cutoff" hypothesis).**

New option: a stretch spliced in by contagion takes only where the k monomers before it match the recipient's (slider
"Matching monomers needed for contagion", 0 = off). This is a mechanism, not a probability curve: divergence lowers
success on its own, as in homologous recombination.

| Variation | Contagion | Matching needed | Species (≥1%) | Effective clusters | Contagions failed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| slow | default (.03) | none | 26, 27 (earlier runs) | 109–144 | — |
| slow | 10× (.3) | none | 20, 15, 13, 8 | 224–599 | — |
| slow | 10× (.3) | 3 monomers | 22, 26, 23, 21 | 146–195 | 51–60% |
| fast (default) | default | none | 4 | 712 | — |
| fast (default) | default | 3 monomers | 3 | 620 | 45% |

- Heavy contagion blurs species. Requiring a matching stretch roughly restores the distinctness seen at default
  contagion. **This supports the cutoff hypothesis:** once sequences diverge, transfer between them fails, and the
  species hold.
- At fast variation the requirement does nothing. There, copying errors, not contagion, make the continuum.

**2. Isolation by place (speciation stage 3).**

Similarity of two cells' sequences by distance apart (slow variation):

| Distance (cells) | <4 | 8–16 | 32–64 | far |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| even | .70 | .36 | .13 | .03 |
| pond | .72 | .48 | .19 | .07 |
| vents | .68 | .43 | .16 | .06 |

- Strong isolation by distance everywhere: species are mostly local clones in patches.
- Habitat barely matters. Among far-apart pairs, the same water temperature gives .13 against .11 (vents), .11 against
  .10 (springs), .09 against .09 (pond). So the page has geographic, not ecological, speciation so far.

**3. Is the lengthening of sequences driven or passive? (McShea's tests)**

David McShea (*Evolution* 48, 1994) separates trends driven by a force from passive diffusion away from a lower bound
(Gould's "left wall"). In a driven trend the minimum rises too, and the whole distribution shifts. In a passive trend the
minimum stays near the bound and the distribution skews right.

| Tick | 1,000 | 4,000 | 8,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| even: minimum / 10th percentile / median | 8 / 20 / 30 | 18 / 30 / 39 | 19 / 33 / 42 |
| pond | 1 / 10 / 20 | 7 / 23 / 32 | 13 / 30 / 38 |

- **The minimum and the lower tail rise, so by McShea's minimum test the trend is driven, not passive.**
- Skew turns negative. This is because sequences pile up at the 48-monomer cap (34% of cells at the cap or within 3 of
  it by tick 8,000 in "even"). **The cap is a right wall, and it now limits the trend.**
- Copying itself is unbiased: the mean change from parent to child is about −0.01 a birth.
- The fecundity part of selection, longer parents against the living, changes sign from one interval to the next. So
  the push seems to come mainly through survival. The likely route is enzymes from buried pairs, but that is not
  tested.
- Removing the cost of length did not speed the trend (mean 38.5 against 41.1 at tick 8,000; one run each).

**4. Why about a third of starts die out.**

| World | Starts that died (of 10–12) |
|---|---|
| even | 1 of 12 |
| vents | 3 of 10 |
| pond | 3 of 10 |
| springs | 3 of 10 |

- Every start goes through a bottleneck at about ticks 10–50, as the random patch of Conway's rule settles into still

- A start survives if a variant that keeps giving birth appears first. Starts that fell below about 15 cells nearly all

**For the theory (proposals).**
- **The cutoff:** distinctness here is held by the failure of exchange, not by a barrier to mating. That fits
  bacterial species better than the biological species concept.
- **The isolation by distance:** this world speciates by milieu in the geographic sense only. Ecological niches are
  not yet strong enough to divide a lineage.
- **Lengthening is driven, but only weakly through births.** It shows the code being pushed toward length by what
  length does in the cell: content shaping expression.

## Domains, first results (12 November; measured, readings are proposals)

Built: a pair and everything folded inside it is a domain. The outer pair names its job; inner pairs of the same kind
add strength; inner pairs of other kinds are held inside and don't act. Whole domains can be duplicated (0.01 a birth),
and contagion can move whole domains (off by default). Runs: 3,000 ticks, 2 per setting, "even" and "pond".

| Setting | Domains a cell | Nested firmer | Light harvest | Uptake | Other enzymes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| domains off (old counting) | 2.2–4.2 | 2–5% | 0.39–1.62 | 1.4–2.5 | ≤ 0.2 |
| domains on | 3.4–5.5 | 2–3% | 0.33–1.22 | 2.1–2.8 | ≤ 0.2 |
| domains on, a fold needs 2 pairs | 2.2–4.0 | 23–38% | 0.00–0.04 | 1.0–2.4 | ~0 |
| domains on, contagion moves domains | 4.4–4.5 | 2–3% | 0.59–0.62 | 2.5 | ≤ 0.2 |

- **As built, domains change little.** Almost every domain is a single pair, because nesting gives no advantage over
  separate pairs. Domain duplication mainly adds more single pairs.
- **When a fold needs 2 same-kind pairs to work, nesting evolves (a quarter to a third of domains),** but only uptake
  is worth building. Light harvest and every other enzyme nearly vanish, and populations stay alive. Biologically, tiny
  folds are unstable, so this rule is the more accurate one, but it costs the page its enzyme diversity.
- **What's missing is a reason for different kinds to nest:** the "coupled jobs" in the doc's Proposal 1, where an
  inner pair of another kind modifies the outer job. That was deferred.
- Species counts did not change.

## Reactions from shape, first results (12 November; measured, readings are proposals)

Built (off by default, "Domains run reactions"): each working domain turns one molecule into another. It takes the
monomer that opens its outer pair and makes (taken + 1 + the sum of the inner pairs' opening monomers) mod 16, so every
inner pair, of any kind, reshapes it. A molecule's energy is the log of its prime: down the ladder yields light, up costs
it. The eight named jobs are switched off. The 48-monomer cap is gone (now 128, so length is limited by copying cost).
Runs: 3,000 ticks, 2 per setting.

| Setting | Population, even | Population, pond | Mean length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| named enzymes (old) | 52–57k | 9.4–10.6k | 34–38 | longest sequences 59–74, past the old cap |
| reactions, with energy | 64–74k | 13.8–41k | 25–39 | most cells burn p → a, the steepest step down |
| reactions, no energy | 48–52k | 5.5–6.1k | 20–26 | they only shuffle molecules |
| reactions, a fold needs 2 pairs | 54k | 7.1–8.2k | 23–25 | pond: nesting evolves (41–47% of domains); even: almost no reactions |

- **Reactions with energy work too well, and for a wrong reason.** Cells find the steepest step down the ladder, the
  lone pair that turns p (53) into a (2), and burn ground molecules for light. That is free energy, because the ground
  regrows every molecule everywhere.
- **Biologically, chemical energy has to come from somewhere.** Chemotrophs live on geochemical disequilibria at vents.
  Heterotrophs live on what other life made. The fix (proposal): the ground regrows only low-energy molecules; high-energy
  ones come from vents and from dead cells.
- **Without energy, reactions do worse than the named enzymes**, since their only use is making scarce building blocks.
- **With the stability rule, nesting evolves for the first time:** larger domains doing energy-yielding conversions.
  Inner pairs of other kinds now matter, since they reshape the product.

## The energy fix (12 November; measured, readings are proposals)

Built: with reactions on, the ground re-forms only low-energy molecules (re-forming falls linearly from a, which is
full, to p, which is none). Vents pour out mostly high-energy ones, and the dead release their molecules as before.
The option is "Energy only from vents and the dead", on whenever reactions are on. Runs: 3,000 ticks, 2 per setting.

| World | Named enzymes (old) | Reactions, free energy (before the fix) | Reactions, with the fix | Species: old → fixed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| even | 52–57k | 64–74k | 39–42k | 1 → 14–19 |
| pond | 9.4–10.6k | 13.8–41k | 7.2–8.4k | 5–9 → 16–17 |
| vents | 0.2–2.5k | — | 12.8–17k | 4–26 → 7–14 |
| springs | 11.9–12.2k | — | 12.7–14.1k | 3 → 15 |

- **The free lunch is gone.** In the even and pond worlds, light gained from reactions now roughly equals, or falls

- **Chemical energy now pays where it should.** At vents, almost every cell burns p into a, and vent worlds go from
  barely surviving to thriving. Cells are five times denser in the squares nearest the vents (9.9 against 2 a square).
- **Species counts rise sharply** in even, pond and springs. A plausible reading: reactions give lineages different
  ways to make a living. Not tested.
- **The cost:** even and pond populations are a fifth to a third lower than with the named enzymes, which gave light
  harvest and uptake for free.
- **In springs, nesting evolved strongly in one run** (64% of domains), with a nested domain turning i into b.

## Step 2, first part: clumps made by their cells (12 November; measured, readings are proposals)

**Built:**
- **The clump code is retired** ("Clumps made by their cells", on). A cell keeps part of what its reactions make on its
  surface as a coat (30%), and larger molecules stick more. Cells whose coat passes 0.2 stick, and a clump is simply a
  connected group of sticky cells. It has no code, division size, engulfing, fusion rule or light pooling. It grows,
  splits and merges as coats come and go.
- **Engulfing is gone**, because it is a eukaryotic trait. It returns with amoebas.
- **Soma is off** in this mode until regulation is built (the next part of step 2).
- **Spores are renamed dormant cells** in all visible text.
- **The cell view is built:** clicking a cell draws it, with its membrane (dashed and thick when dormant), its coat, its
  bonds, its working folds labelled with their reactions, the molecules of its square marked "it takes" or "it makes",
  and a light gauge.

**Results** (3,000 ticks, 2 runs per setting):

| World | Share of cells in clumps: clump code (old) | Made by cells (new) | Relatedness within clumps: old | New |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vents | 77–81% | 11–62% (52–53% with grazers) | 0.42–0.55 | 0.70–0.77 |
| springs | 38–42% | 22–25% | 0.54–0.57 | 0.74–0.86 |
| pond | 22–50% | about 0% | 0.49–0.54 | — |

- **Clumps now form only where chemistry pays for the coat:** vents, then springs. In the pond and the even world they
  all but vanish, and lowering the threshold or raising the coat share didn't bring them back. **Reading:** under the
  old code, clumping was propped up by free benefits, light pooling and a code that cost cells nothing. Paid for by its

- **New clumps are far more related** (0.70–0.86 against 0.42–0.57). They are closer to clonal groups, the fraternal

- **Grazers**: no change in clump share at vents; stickiness in the pond rose a little (2–13% of cells against 0.2–1%).

- **Soma** has not been re-tested: it needs regulation, the next part.

## Step 2, the matrix (12 November; measured, readings are proposals)

**Built, from the sources on real colonies and biofilms:**
- **Retention:** half of what a clump's cells make, and of what its dead release, stays in the clump's matrix, a pool
  its cells draw on first when building. The pool leaks 5% a census back to the ground and spills where the clump
  falls apart.
- **The cost of size:** a clump's cells get 1 / (1 + size/200) of their square's light, from shading and from sinking
  out of the light.

**Already there, and kept:** clump cells resist drying (0.4 of the risk), and grazers can't swallow clumps much bigger
than they are (the gape of 10).

**Results** (3,000 ticks, 1–2 runs per setting):

| World | Share of cells in clumps | Sticky cells | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| pond | 0% | 0.1–0.5% | the matrix did not help |
| pond, with grazers | 0.2–0.6% | 6–11% | grazers raise stickiness, in 4 of 4 runs |
| pond, clumps of 2 count | 3–10% | 4–16% | |
| springs | 4–29% | 15–38% | |
| vents | 0.2–62% | 1–66% | huge variation between runs, with or without the new pieces |
| even | 0.3–1.2% | 7–17% | |

- **The matrix did not rescue clumps in calm water.** Holding molecules for the clump only helps once clumps exist, and
  in the pond almost no cell becomes sticky.
- **Diagnosis** (proposal): the coat comes only from molecular reactions, which calm-water cells rarely run. In real
  colonies the matrix is polysaccharide, sugar made by the cells themselves. In the page, cells that photosynthesise
  make light and sugar, not coat, so they have no way to become sticky.
- **Grazers raise stickiness consistently** (6–11% of cells against 0.1–0.5%). This is the first sign in the page of
  grazer-induced stickiness, as in *Microcystis*, though it doesn't reach the size of a clump.
- **One even-world start died out** (start-up burnout).

**Your idea, 12 November:** flagella would be a nice feature. Motility is not yet in the page beyond drifting.

## Step 2, coat from sugar and the gap rule (12 November; measured, readings are proposals)

## Separating the two models (proposal, for the open choice)

- **The systems model as the general one.** It applies to every system: causal systems, and reproducing systems among
  them. It says what a system is (closure), how systems relate (supersystems, cosystems, subsystems), and how the modelled
  range changes (open, lift, fold).
- **Deleuze and Guattari's strata, with Hjelmslev's net, as an analysis within it.** The net describes how a system's
  content and expression are distributed, once there is expression to distribute. A stratum would then be a region of the
  systems graph marked by one distribution of content and expression:
  - **Causal systems without codes** (the physico-chemical stratum) have content, with at most a relative expression that
    is not detached: the crystal's surface, the convection cell.
  - **The organic stratum** begins where expression detaches as a linear code. In the page, that is the line of
    expression.
  - **The alloplastic stratum** (the human) adds expression that can be read and changed from outside.
- **How the two relate.** Double articulation would describe a system's relation to what it takes in and to its
  subsystems:
  - **The first articulation** chooses and orders matter: content.
  - **The second** builds stable compounds and codes: expression.
  - Open, lift and fold say how the modelled range of systems changes. Strata say what kind of system sits in a region of
    that range.
- **The subject.** On this reading, the subject is the case where one system belongs both to the organic stratum
  (biological supersystems) and to the alloplastic one (sociological supersystems), and mediates between them. That
  extends the user's point that the individual is an intersection.
- **Concern: Rosen.** Rosen proposed closure to efficient causation as what distinguishes organisms, so applying it to
  non-living "causal systems" departs from Rosen. Later work (Moreno and Mossio, on closure of constraints) separates
  self-maintaining physical structures, such as convection cells and flames, from organisms. Either source might give a
  sharper definition of causal systems. **Neither has been checked yet.**

## Sources checked on 11 November, and what they mean for the model

**Moreno and Mossio: closure of constraints.**
- **What closure means.** Biological systems, they argue, differ from other natural systems in realising a closure of
  constraints: a higher-level, emergent regime of causation (Mossio, Bich and Moreno 2013).
- **The flame and the Bénard cell.** These dissipative structures show order, but the constraints that keep them going
  are not themselves produced and repaired by their organisation. That is what separates them from organisms.
- **For the model.** This supports the user's revision. Causal systems need not be closed: dissipative structures (in
  the page, the overturning water) are causal systems without closure. Closure would mark autonomous systems, a
  narrower class, and reproduction a narrower one still. **Proposal:** causal systems ⊃ autonomous (closed) systems ⊃
  reproducing systems.
- **Contested.** Garson's "liberality objection" holds that closure of constraints also applies to many simple,
  non-living systems. Whether supra-organismal systems realise closure is left open.
- **Most relevant to negotiation.** One paper asks, for multi-species partnerships, which system realises closure: the
  partners taken one by one, or the partnership. That is the user's negotiation problem, posed for symbiosis. It bears
  directly on the endosymbiosis stage.

**Negotiation between biology and sociology.** The nearest established frameworks are gene-culture coevolution and niche
construction.
- **Gene-culture coevolution** requires two tracks of inheritance, one genetic and one cultural, each affecting selection
  on the other.
- **Niche construction** treats organisms as co-directors of their own evolution and other species'.
- **The clearest human case is lactase persistence.** The mutations' estimated age brackets the origins of animal
  domestication and dairying. The model is reciprocal: lactase persistence is favoured only in dairying cultures, and
  dairying is favoured more where lactase persistence is common.
- **The user's two directions, in this case.** Genes producing mores, and norms selecting genes, appear together as one
  coevolution, so neither track simply dominates.
- **Proposal: a readout for who dominates.** The Price equation's multilevel form splits the change in a trait into
  selection between groups (the clump) and selection within groups (the cells). Tracked over time, it would show whether
  the clump or its cells drive change, and whether that fluctuates. It would be a measure, not a cause. (The Price
  equation is standard. Its use here is a proposal.)

**The contagion cutoff: supported, with a caveat.**
- **The decline is real.** In bacteria, across widely different genera, the rate of homologous recombination declines
  consistently with genetic divergence. It has been modelled as ρ × 10^(−20π), where π is the divergence between the
  donor and recipient stretches.
- **What a decline does in simulation** (Hanage and colleagues 2006):
  - Where recombination is equally likely between all members, populations move from clonal to sexual structure as the
    recombination rate rises.
  - Clonal populations regularly form clusters that break up or go extinct.
  - In sexual populations, recombination's cohesive force prevents distinct clusters.
  - Where recombination declines log-linearly with genetic distance, distinct clusters can form.
- **The caveat.** The measured decline is not steep enough to produce species in nature under neutral drift alone, so
  other mechanisms are needed.
- **For the page.** Tonight's finding fits the "clonal" case: clusters form when variation is slow. Making contagion's
  success fall with divergence, in the form above, would test the user's cutoff directly.

## If an organisation produces its own constraints, is that expression, and so the net? (the user's question, 11 November; proposal)

**Short answer: nearly, but in a weak form.**

- **Deacon's scale lines up with this.** In *Incomplete Nature* (2011), Terrence Deacon describes three levels:
  - **Homeodynamic:** processes that wear constraints away, toward equilibrium. This may be what lies *outside* the
    causal systems defined so far: processes with no constraint, or only decaying ones. Equilibrium is the limit.
  - **Morphodynamic:** spontaneous order in which form begets form, with no organisation producing the constraint:
    crystals, whirlpools, Bénard convection cells, basalt columns. This would be causal systems without closure.
  - **Teleodynamic:** two morphodynamic processes coupled so that each constrains and regenerates the other. His
    simplest case, the "autogen", is an autocatalytic cycle that makes the membrane enclosing it. This would be closure.
- **The net is already present at the first stratum.** For Deleuze and Guattari every stratum, the physico-chemical
  included, has content and expression. Below the organic stratum, though, expression is not independent: it is
  coextensive with the stratum. The crystal's form is the expression of its molecular content, but it expresses only at
  its surface, and it develops by **induction** from layer to layer. So spontaneous order (morphodynamics) already has
  expression in this weak sense, induced from outside or at the surface.
- **Closure would be where expression starts being produced from within.** When constraints are produced and repaired by
  the organisation they constrain, the constraint is no longer induced from outside: it is made inside, and it shapes
  the processes that make it. This parallels Deleuze and Guattari's contrast between the crystal's induction and the
  organic stratum's **transduction**. It also parallels their **interior milieu**. On this reading, closure is a
  threshold within the net, not its beginning.
- **The code is the further threshold:** expression detached as an independent linear line, the organic stratum proper.
- **Proposal: three thresholds of expression.**
  1. **Induced** (spontaneous order).
  2. **Self-produced but coextensive with content** (closure).
  3. **Detached and linear** (the code).

  The page has examples of each:
  - the overturning water: induced;
  - a clump that maintains its own slime, sharing and shape: arguably closure;
  - the line of expression: the code.
- **Caution.** A constraint, for Montévil and Mossio, is not a sign. It is a local, contingent cause that reduces the
  degrees of freedom of the processes it acts on, and that stays conserved over their time scale. Reading constraint as
  expression is an interpretation that fits Deleuze and Guattari's broad, non-semiotic use of "expression". It is not
  something these authors claim.

**Chemistry audit, part 1: conservation of matter (13 November, at your go-ahead).**

**Test.** A closed dish, where nothing should make or destroy molecules. Count the ground, the cells' compounds and
the clumps' pools.
**Result:** total molecules rose 15% in 600 ticks.

**Found by accounting for every change to the ground** (test/ground.py):
1. **produce() makes matter from energy.** Cells with the producer gene (quorum-gated) add molecules of their own
   compound's kinds to their square, up to the ground's cap, paying only a little energy. In 30 ticks it made about
   34,000 molecules; it is the whole of the rise.
2. **Death returns 70% of a cell's compound** (chrecyc). The other 30% vanishes.
3. **Birth charges a lonely cell extra molecules,** which are destroyed. This is a leftover of the Life rule
   (1 + 0.14 × missing neighbours).
4. **Contagion rewrites a neighbour's compound** with no molecules changing hands.
5. **Coat from reactions** turns part of a reaction's product molecules into coat, which is counted as energy.
   Matter becomes energy.

**These conserve:**
- the ground's own step (a change of a millionth in 50 steps);
- births: newborns get exactly the compound they paid for;
- reactions' transfers;
- the clumps' pools;
- mutation, which happens only at birth;
- predation, which releases the prey like any other death.

**Proposed fixes (not yet made):**
- **1:** production draws its material from sugar (a carbon source) or converts other molecules, as a reaction does.
- **2:** a death returns everything: to the ground, the matrix, or, in the dish, to the remains, which release it as
  they break down.
- **3:** remove the fee.
- **4:** the rewritten cell pays or receives the difference from its square, or the write fails.
- **5:** keep coat as matter, made of molecules.
- **Then:** the energy audit (light, sugar, reactions, coat).

**Fixes made (13 November, at your go-ahead):**
1. **Production** builds molecules out of sugar, one for one.
2. **A death returns the whole compound.** In the dish the remains hold it and release it as they break down.
3. **The lonely-birth fee** applies only with the Life rule on.
4. **Contagion** pays or receives the difference from the square, or fails.
5. **Coat is matter.** Each cell keeps a ledger of what its coat is made of: sugar (coat from sugar now uses sugar,
   plus a little energy for building it) and product molecules of one kind. Shed coat and a dead cell's coat go back
   to the ground.

**Retest (closed dish, sugar 20, 600 ticks, test/mass2.py):**

- Carbon (sugar, CO2 and coat) falls, because CO2 escapes into the air above the open dish: a real outflow.
- **Withdrawn:** the claim above that death sugar (ebody) is made from nothing. Birth already fixes the body's 0.2 of
  carbon from CO2 and takes its water from the square, so the 0.2 of sugar and the water a death releases are the

**Chemistry audit, part 2: energy (13 November, at your go-ahead).**
- **Units.** A unit of sugar is a unit of energy and of carbon: photosynthesis turns light into sugar one for one,
  using CO2, and respiration turns sugar back into CO2. Molecules hold RXE energy each.
- **Breaches found and fixed:**
  1. **The reserve multiplied energy by 4.** Banking 1 unit stored 4 (rconv), and withdrawing gave all 4 back as
     energy. Now withdrawal returns what was banked.
  2. **Uphill reactions cost too little.** Making a higher-energy molecule cost 0.02 per unit of energy difference,
     while the molecules gained 0.05. Now the cost is the energy stored divided by the downhill efficiency (0.4),
     so a round trip loses energy, as it should.
  3. **Moving copied energy.** A gliding cell left a copy of its energy behind; a hopping cell traded its own for
     whatever lay at the new place. Now the cell and the place it left swap.

  symmetric. About 0.4 of energy gained in 1.7 million over 20 ticks.

**Built: the folded record (13 November, at your go-ahead).** It meets your rule that a fold summarises and does not

**Built: short chains in the ground (13 November, your decision).** Switch: "Fragments in the ground", on by default.
- **Release:** a dying cell may release (30% chance) a fragment of 3 to 8 monomers from its line of expression. The
  fragment is built from the ground's molecules, one per monomer, so matter is conserved. Each square holds up to 4
  kinds of fragment, each with a number of copies.
- **Breakdown:** about 0.2% a tick, returning the molecules.
- **Catalysis:** a fragment that folds into a working domain runs its reaction in the ground without being used up.
  Downhill only, since no cell pays; the energy released is lost as heat.
- **Uptake (as in natural transformation; checked against the usual account of B. subtilis, where taken-up DNA mostly
  replaces a matching stretch by homologous recombination):** an active cell may take a fragment up. It replaces the
  best-matching stretch of the cell's sequence, if at least half of it matches. If the cell's compound changes, it pays
  or receives the difference in molecules, or the uptake fails.

**Built: joining and catalysed joining of chains in the ground (13 November, at your go-ahead).**
- **Joining:** two copies in a square link into one longer copy, spending one high-energy molecule (one of the four
  highest, which becomes its low-energy counterpart), as activation is spent in real ligation. Monomers are exactly
  conserved. Each square makes one try per kind of chain present, every 8 ticks.
- **Ligases:** a chain whose fold pairs a monomer with its partner (the chaining fold cells use for coat) speeds up
  joining in its square (fragLig).
- **Breaking:** at a random bond, into two pieces; more often the longer the chain (per bond). Pieces under 3 monomers
  return as molecules.
- **Limits:** chains of up to 32 monomers; up to 8 kinds per square.
- **Defaults** (my choice after a short sweep): joining 0.3, breaking 0.0005 per bond per tick.

**Results (vent world, 2,000 ticks, one run per setting):**
- **At first, joining was rare** and chains stopped at 16 monomers, two released fragments joined once: they broke
  before joining again. This is the real difficulty of chains in water before life (the "water problem").
- **With slower breaking and one try per kind:**
  - about 10,000 joins in 2,000 ticks;
  - longest chains 21 to 24 monomers;
  - 8,000 to 10,000 distinct sequences present at once;
  - chains with the chaining fold rose about sixfold over the run (100 to 800 slots), in both settings. Possibly
    joining favouring the folds that speed it, but this is not tested.
- **Mean length stays about 5.** Without copying, a long chain is a one-off that eventually breaks, so the ground's
  variety does not run away yet.
- **Conservation:** in the closed dish, molecules rose 0.2% in 300 ticks, all of it from production. It holds.

**Built: templated copying (13 November, at your go-ahead).**
- **Copying:** a chain lines up free monomers against itself, each with its partner (position ^ 8). The new strand runs
  the other way, as real strands are antiparallel, so copying the copy gives back the original.
- **Materials:** one molecule per monomer from the ground. Energy: one high-energy molecule per bond.
- **Errors:** each monomer may be miscopied (fragErr 0.02).
- **Catalysis:** the template is not used up; ligase chains in the square speed copying. Rate fragCopy 0.05 per kind
  per 8 ticks, scaled down for long chains.

**Result (vent world, 3,000 ticks, one run):**
- About 40,000 copies, about 9% with an error.
- **The most abundant chains changed.** Early: bccc, bcc, ccc (cheap low-energy monomers). From tick 1,500:
  php, hph, phph, still growing at tick 3,000.
  - php and hph are each other's copy; phph is its own (self-complementary).
  - So selection for copying arose outside cells, with no rule for it.
- **Chains that pair partners fold into the chaining fold,** which is the ligase. Ligase slots rose from about 50 to
  about 1,060. So the best copiers may speed up the joining and copying in their squares: a possible small
  autocatalytic loop (Kauffman). Not tested directly.
- **No takeover:** the top sequence holds 5% of all copies, rising slowly; about 10,000 distinct sequences remain;
  chains reach the 32-monomer limit; the cells are unaffected (about 32,000).
- **Conservation holds:** closed dish, 300 ticks, +0.1%, from production.

- Cells copying ground chains, and chains entering cells: the step to selfish elements and viruses.

## Lehninger on protein structure, compared with the line of expression (14 November)

Source: Justin's extract `biochemchapters3-6.pdf` (Drive). The text that came through covers chapter 3 and chapter 4 up to the start of quaternary structure; section 4.4 (denaturation and folding), chapter 5 and chapter 6 were cut off by the Drive reader. The pages appear to be from an earlier edition than the 8th (references run to about 2001).

What the page already matches (no change proposed):
- Sequence alone sets the fold (infold is deterministic).
- Domains: large chains fold into units that keep their shape when cut out; the page's outermost brackets are domains, and duplication and contagion move whole domains.
- Many sequences give the same fold; structure is conserved more than sequence (the page counts neutral mutations).

Proposals (Claude's, awaiting Justin's decision):
1. Marginal stability. A fold is held by many weak interactions against the chain's entropy, and the margin is small. Give each domain a stability score (pairs held minus a cost for its length) and let heat subtract from it, so denaturation and heat tolerance emerge instead of being a named job.
2. Two kinds of monomer. Burying oily groups is the main driving force; an unpartnered charged group inside a core is badly destabilising. Split the 16 monomers into oily and polar/charged classes that count towards stability. Bears on the open question of how many kinds per monomer: a few classes of property may matter more than many kinds.
3. Local rules. Neighbouring residues decide whether a stretch can coil (a run of like charges cannot). A run of one class could be barred from pairing.
4. Cofactors. Some proteins need a non-amino-acid group (a metal, a heme) to work. A domain could need a molecule from its square, tying folds to the ground chemistry.
5. Subunits. Chains that assemble, with binding of a small molecule changing how they fit, are the basis of regulation. Deferred.
6. A readout: distinct folds or reactions against distinct sequences in the population.

### Decision (Justin, 14 November)
- Adopt proposals 1 and 2: domains are only marginally stable (stability score, heat lowers it), and monomers carry polarity (oily vs polar/charged), which is also needed for realistic cell walls.

### From the rest of chapter 4 and chapter 6 (Lehninger, read 14 November)
- Heat unfolding is abrupt over a narrow range (a melting temperature): unfolding is cooperative. Heat-stable proteins of hot-spring bacteria often differ only slightly from ordinary ones.
- Denatured chains of some proteins refold on their own when conditions return (Anfinsen): the sequence holds all the folding information.
- Folding is not a random search (Levinthal); one model is hierarchical: local structure first, then longer-range packing, then domains. The page's bracket nesting already folds this way.
- Chapter 6: a good enzyme fits the transition state, not the substrate; binding energy from many weak contacts lowers the barrier and gives specificity. Many enzymes need cofactors (metal ions, coenzymes).

### Proposal: 3D folds (Claude's, awaiting decision)
- Tier 1, a viewer: draw the selected cell's chain folded in 3D in the inspector, coloured oily/polar, from the existing bracket fold. Display only.
- Tier 2, a lattice model: fold each sequence as a self-avoiding chain on a 3D grid, scoring oily-oily contacts (Dill's HP model, 1985). Stability = contacts; heat = a Metropolis temperature; the active site = a pocket of exposed residues. Costly (finding the best fold is NP-hard), so fold once per distinct sequence and cache; the GPU session could fold in batches.

## How fine-grained: atoms, real chemistry, or beads? (Justin's question, 14 November; Claude's proposal)
- **All-atom simulation with real chemistry is out of reach for evolving populations.** Folding even small, fast proteins
  this way took a special-purpose supercomputer and simulations of 100 µs to 1 ms (Lindorff-Larsen et al. 2011).
  Real-chemistry force fields are themselves fitted approximations of quantum mechanics, so they are folds too.
- **Proposal: coarse-grained "beads".** Each monomer is built from a few typed beads, in the manner of the Martini force
  field: about four heavy atoms per bead; bead types charged, polar, intermediate, apolar.

    - weak interactions are roughly 10–100 times weaker than covalent bonds;
    - a folded protein is only marginally more stable than unfolded;

  uncertainty. Where it is uncertain (a new sequence, conditions outside its training), the detailed level is opened,
  run, and added to the training data (active learning). That is the reopening rule of the open-and-fold decision.
- First case: proteins (sequence → stability at T, rate, specificity), after the two-bead folder works. Second: the
  population scale (local conditions + cells' folded traits → per-patch rates), after task 22's per-cell model.
- An emulator is a fold: it is validated against detailed runs it has not seen, and its outputs are measures, not
  causes.

  - fold only: pond 1 of 3, springs 0 of 2;
  - with the starting pigment and inheritance: pond 3 of 3 (5,000–11,000 cells at tick 1,000, against about 20,000

**The decay economy** (`detOn`, default on; `detBreak` 0.05, `detLeach` 0.001, `detRot` 0.002; `detDie()`, `detStep()`).
- **Found:** before, a dying cell's stored energy was lost; only a fixed 0.2 of sugar was released.
- **Now:**
  - the stored energy becomes remains in the square, which do not spread;
  - remains leach and rot slowly;
  - mostly, they are broken down into sugar by the square's heterotrophs, in proportion to their het levels. The
    sugar is shared.
- About 93% of remains return as sugar.
- **Result: no shift towards a mixed community in the pond.** Cells without pigment but with heterotrophy are 1–7%,
  with or without remains, and also with dissolved-sugar rot cut from 0.02 to 0.005.
  - So in the pond the limit is not sugar but space and light: carrying pigment is cheap against what light gives, so
    nearly every cell keeps it (mixotrophs).
  - The hot springs do give mixed communities, with 30–67% non-pigmented heterotrophs in some runs, with or without
    remains.
- **Proposal:** look for why the springs mix and the pond does not (shade, temperature bands, or the source's chemistry)
  before adding more ecology.

## Decision (Justin, 15 November, about 22:42): from one chain to a genome of genes
- Justin decided to follow this line of development. Today each cell has one line of up to 128 monomers that is at once:
  - its genome (copied, mutated, transferred);
  - its single protein (up to 4 working domains);
  - its identity (the unpaired compound).
- The planned stages (Claude's proposal of the order; each stage is Justin's decision when it comes):
  1. **Several genes per cell:** the line becomes a genome of separate genes, each folding into its own chain. This
     gives many functions, and quaternary structure later.
  2. **Expression levels:** a copy number or rate per gene, and switches on genes, so regulation can evolve.
  3. **Storage separate from function:** the heritable form and the folded working form become two things, as DNA and
     protein are.
- **Timing:** the page uses real folds only once the two-bead folder passes (tasks 23, 26) and its summaries can be
  learned (task 24). The design of stage 1 can be written now (cloud session), to be built once folds are ready.
- Fit with folding: each gene's chain is folded and cached separately. Many short chains are easier to fold and to learn
  than one long one, so stage 1 also helps tasks 24 and 26.

## Following the genes over longer runs (cloud session, 16 November, about 09:00–09:20)
Measurements on the stage-1 page, soup start, seed 1, 6,000 ticks, sampled every 1,000 (test/genes_long.py). "Near
copies" are two same-length genes in one cell differing at a quarter of their monomers or fewer.

| | even world, tick 1,000 → 6,000 | vent world, tick 1,000 → 6,000 |
|---|---|---|
| cells with more than one gene | 41% → 46% | 85% → 95% |
| genes a cell (most in one cell) | 1.57 → 1.64 (6 → 11) | 2.81 → 3.57 (9 → 15) |
| mean length (longest) | 14.2 → 22.5 (79 → 256) | 55 → 194 (256 from tick 1,000) |
| monomers that are markers | 21% → 14% | 14% → 6% |
| exact-copy gene pairs | 20,164 → 14,326 | 12,277 → 2,894 |
| near copies (share running a different reaction) | 3,809 (20%) → 3,530 (35%) | 10,381 (39%) → 3,381 (40%) |
| reactions a cell; cells running more than 4 | 3.15 → 3.87; 16% → 25% | 5.1 → 7.3; 54% → 92% |
| genes duplicated / lost / passed / from viruses, total | 12,527 / 5,638 / 31,715 / 28 | 17,527 / 23,163 / 74,554 / 12,916 |

Findings (measures, not causes):
- **Genomes grow.** Slowly in the even world, speeding up after tick 4,000; fast in the vent world, where the 256 cap
  is reached by tick 1,000 and the mean is near it by 6,000. In the vent world losses now outnumber duplications,
  which is what pressing against the cap would look like.
- **Duplicates diverge into new jobs.** Exact copies fall over time; at any sample a few thousand near copies exist, and
  a third or more of them run a different reaction from their twin. This is the duplication-then-divergence route.
- **Virus chains join genomes**, rarely in the even world (28) and often in the vent world (12,916), where the
  epidemics are. Whether they stay is not yet measured: genes carry no record of where they came from.
- **Markers are not overused overall.** The share falls as genes lengthen. Single cells can be high (one 5-gene cell
  had 18 marker monomers in 52).
- **Repeated reactions.** Cells often carry the same reaction several times (one gene: l→m four times). Each copy runs,
  so the copies raise that reaction's rate. This looks like gene dosage (more copies, more product), which is real
  biology, but it was not designed for.
- **Transfer accelerates** (even world: 212 by tick 1,000, 31,715 by 6,000), as kin genomes become similar enough to
  splice.
- **Test caveat:** two runs with the same seed gave 81,927 and 89,309 cells at tick 1,000. The page likely runs frames
  before the test stops it. Seeded comparisons need the harness to stop the page first; worth telling the GPU session
  for task 29.

Proposals (Claude's, for Justin):
1. Is the 256 cap the right limit, or should length cost more? The copying cost is 0.0004 light a monomer.
2. Tag each gene with its origin (own, transferred, virus) for display only, to see whether virus genes persist.
3. Is the dosage effect of repeated reactions wanted, or should identical reactions share one rate?

The inspector now lists each gene: its monomers, domains (and adaptors), reactions with strength, and whether it is an
exact copy of another gene in the cell. Reactions past the cell's 8 are struck through. The summary has a "Genes:"
line (genes a cell, share multi-gene, marker share, exact copies, and the running totals).

## Decisions (Justin, 16 November, about 09:37–10:06)
1. **Genome proposals approved** (from "Following the genes"):
   - length: a small running cost for each working domain each tick (option c); a higher cap later, on the desktop (d);
   - origin tags on genes (own line, another cell, virus), for display only;
   - repeated reactions keep adding speed, but each copy pays the running cost (option d).
2. **The founder is upgraded as features arrive.** The baseline is what is common to all modern life, not early life:
   the project is not simulating the origin of life. Copying by pairing is kept folded most of the time (a rate and an
   error rate set by a copier gene), and opened when needed.
3. **A modern-baseline founder, all five parts approved** (order still to be set):
   1. 4-letter storage, translated by a three-letter code (64 codons) into the working letters; the translation is
      folded into a code table the cell carries and can mutate;
   2. a copier gene (copying by pairing, folded);
   3. a membrane with transporters (uptake through genes, not a rule);
   4. an ion gradient driving an ATP-synthase-like gene (with the ions already decided);
   5. simplified building-block metabolism (precursors a cell must make or take up before copying).
   Source for the baseline: Goldman and Kaçar 2022 (LUCA's genome and proteome).
4. **One page, two jobs.** The page stays where everything is assembled and watched; the GPU's detailed models reach it
   folded (task 24 emulators). Two founders as a setting: the simple founder kept for from-scratch tests, the modern one
   the default for long runs. Builders: cloud does items 1 and 2, the folded forms of 3–5, and the founder setting;
   GPU does the detailed models behind 3–5 and their emulators.
5. **The GPU version records, then replays** (Justin's design). The model runs for a set number of ticks headless, then
   produces a page like the live one that can be paused, played forward and back to the last tick, and inspected,
   without the lag of running live. Claude's proposed implementation:
   - full snapshots every 100–500 ticks (about 10–20 MB compressed each); a light display frame every tick (about
     20–100 KB compressed), streamed in chunks;
   - inspecting a cell jumps to the nearest earlier snapshot and re-runs up to the tick;
   - changing the world (painting, settings) makes a branch: continue live from the snapshot, or send it back to the GPU;
   - needs exact repeatability (same seed, same run: currently failing, see "Following the genes"), and one codebase
     (the page's own code run headless in Node on the desktop).

Proposals (Claude's, not yet decided): keep the page in JavaScript rather than port it to Python (plain Python is
slower, and the page's sequential random-order updates do not parallelise without changing the dynamics); run long and
many-seed runs headless in Node; move only diffusion and fields to the graphics card if timing shows they dominate.
